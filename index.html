Astrophotography Satellite Trail Predictor — User Guide

Understanding the Sky View FOV Window

The Sky View represents a 7.1° x 4.0° snippet of the altitude/azimuth grid centered directly over your selected target object.

  • Blue Box: Represents the true sensor Field of View (FOV) of your chosen smart telescope (DWARF II/3, Seestar, Vespera, Unistellar, etc.).
  • Center Ellipse: Scaled accurately to match the selected target's angular size (e.g. Andromeda M31 is ~3° long, Orion M42 is ~1° wide).
  • Red Vector Line: Passes directly through the deep-sky target body. Sub-exposures during this exact minute will contain satellite streaks.
  • Orange Vector Line: Passes through your sensor's FOV border but misses the bright target core.
  • Purple Vector Line: Near-miss satellite transit inside the 7.1° x 4.0° surrounding sky region.

Full Night Density & Shooting Windows

Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite passes peak near dusk and dawn when sunlight illuminates satellites against dark skies. Mid-night hours feature fewer illuminated passes due to Earth's shadow cone.

  • Interactive Box: Click and drag the box to select your planned shooting session hours.
  • Resize Handles: Drag the left or right edges to shorten or lengthen session duration.
  • Snap Window Here: Automatically positions your shooting session over the lowest-density satellite gap for maximum frame efficiency.

Efficiency & Sub-Exposure Math

Shorter sub-exposures preserve more clean exposure time! When a satellite streaks across the frame during a 300-second exposure, you lose 5 full minutes of data. With 10-second sub-exposures, you only lose 10 seconds.

Efficiency % = [(Total Sub-Exposures - Corrupted Sub-Exposures) / Total Sub-Exposures] * 100

Deep Sky vs Widefield Best Practices

Deep Sky Imaging (DSO)

Use rejection stacking algorithms like Kappa-Sigma Clipping or Winsorized Sigma Clipping in Siril, PixInsight, or DSS to automatically strip satellite trails without discarding raw subs.

Widefield & Timelapses

Satellite trails cannot be easily removed from single-frame widefield or Milky Way timelapses. Use this tool to shoot during low-density time windows!
