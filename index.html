Astrophotography Satellite Predictor Guide

1. Physics of Satellite Illumination & LEO Geometry

Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites circle Earth at altitudes between 300 km and 1,200 km. During astronomical twilight and early night hours, while observers on Earth's surface are in darkness, satellites at high altitudes remain directly illuminated by sunlight. As Earth rotates deeper into night, satellites enter the Earth's umbral shadow cone, rendering them invisible to optical telescopes.

2. Sky View Focal Plane & Liang-Barsky Vector Truncation

The central Sky View canvas represents a centered focal field centered on your selected Messier object in Horizon (Alt/Az) coordinates. The central blue box outlines the exact Field of View (FOV) of your selected smart telescope.

  • Red Lines (Through Target): Satellite trajectory crosses directly through the target celestial body boundary.
  • Orange Lines (Across Frame): Satellite enters the telescope sensor rectangular FOV box.
  • Purple Lines (Near-Miss): Trajectory passes within 1.5° of the field center without entering sensor frame.

3. Night Timeline & Optimal Gap Window Search

The timeline spans from evening dusk to morning dawn. Vertical tick marks represent predicted satellite passes across time. The mint green highlighted zone (#30D158) automatically locates the longest contiguous interval without any FOV crossings. Clicking "Snap Window to Optimal Gap" immediately centers your session over this clean interval.

4. Sub-Exposure Metrics & Rejection Algorithms

Stacking software (e.g., DeepSkyStacker, PixInsight, Siril) uses pixel rejection routines like Kappa-Sigma Clipping or Winsorized Sigma Clipping to eliminate satellite streaks. However, when exposure durations are too long, a bright streak damages a higher percentage of total exposures. Choosing shorter sub-exposures (e.g., 10s–30s vs 300s) maximizes total usable integration time.

5. Active Satellites & Telemetry Lookup

Clicking any row in the active satellite table opens an operational telemetry popup displaying NORAD ID, satellite owner/country, launch date, orbit classification, and visual magnitude.
